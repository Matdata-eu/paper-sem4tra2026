# CDL 2026 — 3-Minute Pitch Video Script

**Format:** 9:16 vertical · story format · ≤3:00 · publicly accessible link
**Goal:** convey enthusiasm, context and fit — *not* a demo or screen capture.
**Delivery tip:** speak to camera, energetic and warm. Target ~430 words (comfortably under
3:00 at a natural pace). Timings are cues, not hard cuts.

---

## Title card (on screen, 0:00–0:03)
> **Building the Norwegian Railway Knowledge Graph, where the Semantic Web Stack Runs Off the Track**

---

## [0:03 – 0:25] Hook — the problem, made human

"Picture an entire national railway — thousands of kilometres of track, signals, switches,
bridges, tunnels. Now picture that the *same* railway exists in a dozen different computer
systems… and every one of them disagrees. Different maps, different names, different
reality. Change something in one, and the others never find out."

"That's not a bug. That's how most of the world's critical railway infrastructure is actually run
today."

"I'm a knowledge-graph practitioner, and I spent the last few years helping the Norwegian
infrastructure manager, BaneNOR, fixing exactly this — for real and in production."

"Today our graph holds about **thirteen million facts**, across **five thousand kilometres**
of track and gave us the honor of being the first infra manager to deliver micro topology data
to the european register of railway infrastructure."

"Here's an interesting insight. Most people think 'railway on a map' means geometry — dots and
lines, latitude and longitude. But a railway isn't really about *where* things are. It's
about **how they connect**. Which track leads to which. Which signal allows routing the train to your platform.
What actually matters is **topology**: the network as a graph."

"A topology graph in a knowledge graph actually solves more problems than you might think."

"When we tried to do this with the standard Semantic Web stack — GeoSPARQL, SHACL — we hit
a wall. These tools are pretty good at *geometry*. But they have almost no idea about
*topology*, *routing*, or *linear referencing* — the things railways are actually made of.
So a huge chunk of our logic had to escape the graph and live in SQL instead."

"That gap isn't just a railway problem. It's every network — utilities, telecoms,
logistics, supply chains."

"In my talk, I'll show you the pipeline that got us here, the mistakes we had to undo, where 
the graph tech shines — and where it still needs a push. If you build knowledge graphs for 
anything shaped like a network, this one's for you."

"Thank you and see you in London."

## Production notes
- **Aspect ratio 9:16** — shoot vertical (phone in portrait is fine). Frame head-and-shoulders.
- Keep on-screen text short and high-contrast for mobile viewing.
- One clean take beats a polished slideshow — CDL asked for a *speaker preview*, not a demo.
- Optional B-roll (very light): a station/track shot for the hook, a simple node-and-edge
  graph animation at the "topology" beat. Don't let visuals bury your face — enthusiasm is
  what's being scored.
- Host on YouTube (unlisted is fine if the link is publicly accessible), Vimeo, or Loom;
  confirm the link opens in an incognito window before submitting.
- **Trim to under 3:00.** If long, cut the utilities/telecoms aside at 2:10 first.
